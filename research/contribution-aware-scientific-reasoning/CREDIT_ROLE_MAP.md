# CRediT Role Map for Research Planning and Attribution

**Source of definitions:** https://credit.niso.org/contributor-roles-defined/

**Implementation guidance:** https://credit.niso.org/implementing-credit/

CRediT formally describes **14 contribution roles**. The scientific questions and proposed verification examples in the third column below are **our project extension**, not language or requirements issued by NISO.

| Official CRediT role | Core scope from CRediT (paraphrased) | Research use and evidence examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conceptualization | Formulating research aims and ideas | Dated hypotheses, problem framing, competing mechanism proposals |
| Data curation | Annotating, cleaning, organizing and preserving usable data | Source manifests, timestamps, sensor definitions, missing-data records, checksums |
| Formal analysis | Statistical, mathematical, computational or other formal analysis | Mathematical derivations, model fits, uncertainty and sensitivity results |
| Funding acquisition | Obtaining the project's financial support | Award records, supported period and project, not assumed authorship |
| Investigation | Performing research experiments and collecting data or evidence | Experiment protocols, measurements, executed simulation evidence as applicable |
| Methodology | Designing research methods and models | Governing equations, assumptions, model architecture, test plan |
| Project administration | Coordinating planning and execution | Research plans, assigned work, meeting records and tracked deliverables |
| Resources | Supplying materials, datasets, instrumentation and computational capacity | Equipment logs, computing access, data permissions, resource inventory |
| Software | Writing and implementing code, programs and algorithms; testing code components | Code commits, release hashes, tests, run logs and dependencies |
| Supervision | Oversight, leadership and mentorship of research | Substantive documented guidance, critical method reviews and supervision records |
| Validation | Verification of reproducibility/replication of results, experiments and outputs | Independent re-execution and result agreement; document separately whether physical validation occurred |
| Visualization | Creating or presenting research graphics and visual information | Figure source data, plotting code, labels, uncertainties and review |
| Writing – original draft | Preparing an original scholarly text | Versioned authorship of initial passages or manuscript |
| Writing – review & editing | Critically reviewing, commenting on or revising scholarly writing | Tracked substantive revisions, expert criticism and responses |

## Attribution rules

1. One researcher can hold multiple roles and a single role can involve multiple people.
2. Assign only roles backed by concrete work; document work and output.
3. Optional **lead, equal, supporting** descriptions must be explicitly labeled **project or publisher-specific**. They are not currently part of formal CRediT.
4. Confirm the contribution assignments with participants where practicable. Record disagreements rather than silently imposing a narrative.
5. Separate contribution from authorship. Authorship and acknowledgment decisions follow the applicable institutional or publisher policy, not this table.
6. Never enter identifiable contribution records into a public repository unless publication and attribution are authorized.

## Caution about the Validation role

In the official taxonomy, **Validation** concerns verification of replication and reproducibility of research outputs. In physics and engineering, **physical model validation** is a narrower evidential question about comparison to appropriate independent observations under specified conditions.

A contributor may correctly receive the CRediT role **Validation** for independently reviewing reproducibility while the mathematical model's physical applicability remains unvalidated. Preserve this distinction in both registers.

## Relation to scientific judgments

- A correct derivation does not establish that the constitutive law applies to a particular transformer.
- A solver test does not demonstrate connected EVSE measurements.
- An independent computer rerun does not automatically validate a causal physical pathway.
- A donor's funding contribution need not imply direct scientific supervision, and does not itself determine journal authorship.
- A scientific finding does not become inaccurate because different people contributed at different stages.

**Use the CRediT vocabulary for contribution accounting; use separately qualified evidence for truth claims.**
