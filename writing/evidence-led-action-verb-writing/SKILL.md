# Evidence-Led Action-Verb Writing Skill

## Purpose and governing rule

Choose **the strongest action verb that the evidence can justify**, not the strongest-sounding verb.

This skill governs wording and scientific integrity, not the underlying truth of a scientific claim. Its purpose is to express exactly **what was done, how, by whom, with what evidence or result, and under which limitations**, without replacing the author's authentic voice.

Use for thesis chapters, research articles, reports, conference abstracts, presentations, CVs, statements of purpose, scholarship applications, recommendations when authorized, research biographies, GitHub READMEs, technical documentation, teaching, mentoring, and community work.

## Authoritative seven-rule writing standard

1. **Use precise action verbs supported by evidence.** Choose words such as *formulated, derived, developed, quantified, evaluated, verified, validated,* or *implemented* according to what was actually accomplished.
2. **Demonstrate accomplishment, not merely responsibility.** Describe the action, method, contribution, measurable result, and important limitation.
3. **Maintain scientific rigor.** Distinguish theoretical derivation, numerical simulation, empirical observation, verification, validation, and proposed research. Never exaggerate novelty, causation, impact, or performance.
4. **Use quantitative evidence appropriately.** Preserve accurate numbers, units, sample sizes, comparison baselines, uncertainty, and scientific limitations.
5. **Protect the author's authentic voice.** Write clearly and naturally, preserving chronology, personal reasoning, and ownership of contributions. Avoid generic promotional language and em dashes.
6. **Respect all protected wording.** Never change an immutable passage or any content outside the explicitly requested editing scope.
7. **Verify the finished document.** Check the accuracy of every important verb, result, attribution, and conclusion before presenting it.

**Governing principle: Every important claim must be defensible through the evidence available.**

## Evidence-first writing procedure

1. Identify the requested genre, intended audience and precise authorized edit scope.
2. Consult the controlling source files or statements. Extract actor, actual contribution, method, tools, data, sample/denominator, units, comparator, time horizon, experimental conditions, uncertainty and outcome.
3. Label the evidence and scientific status of every material claim before choosing a verb.
4. Select an action verb only at the level demonstrated. The recommended verb bank is in [ACTION_VERB_BANK.md](references/ACTION_VERB_BANK.md).
5. Compose as **action → object → method → evidence/result → essential limitation**, when appropriate to genre. Avoid list-like SOP paragraphs.
6. Distinguish measured effects, simulation, inference, planned activity and qualified author contribution. Preserve negative and mixed outcomes.
7. Run the quality checklist. Return the requested draft without unauthorized changes; flag evidence gaps rather than inventing facts.

## Evidence maturity and permissible verbs

| Level | What evidence establishes | Examples of accurate verbs | Never infer from this level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S0 Proposed | Hypothesis, plan or candidate only | proposed, hypothesized, outlined, targeted | Completed work, effect or validation |
| S1 Formulated | Defined framework, model specification, assumption or derivation | defined, formulated, derived, specified | Implemented code or measured impact |
| S2 Implemented | Actual artifact, executable code, conducted audit, operational procedure | implemented, built, programmed, documented, audited | Improved outcome without comparison |
| S3 Analyzed / numerically tested | Original or synthetic computation with recorded method and results | simulated, evaluated, quantified, compared, estimated, numerically verified | Physical causality, broadly validated field operation |
| S4 Independently substantiated | Suitable independent numerical reproduction or empirical observations of the same response and domain | independently reproduced, corroborated, physically validated within [domain] | Universality, regulatory certification, guaranteed safety |
| S5 Demonstrated in use | Documented use with measurable attribution and relevant baseline | deployed, installed, delivered, achieved, reduced, increased | Impacts not actually measured or attributable |

These are **claim-specific levels**, not an overall rank of a person or project. A mathematically verified solver can coexist with an unvalidated physical model.

### Verb qualification rules

- **Derived** means a mathematical or logical relationship was obtained under stated premises; it does not mean experimentally proven.
- **Verified** must identify what was checked: calculation, numerical implementation, data hash, constraints, or artifact structure. State against what independent reference and within what tolerance.
- **Validated** requires appropriate independent observations against the physical or operational claim, including quantity, conditions and applicability limits. It is not interchangeable with numerical convergence, fitting or calibration.
- **Reproduced** requires a faithful methodological and input replay and a comparison with the originally reported result; **replicated** generally involves independent implementation, data or evidence.
- **Quantified** requires an actual quantity, unit/denominator and method. It need not imply causation.
- **Optimized** requires a defined objective and constraints with evidence for the claimed optimality; otherwise use formulated, scheduled, evaluated or improved under a stated comparator.
- **Outperformed** requires an identified comparator and an explicitly qualified evaluation metric. One improved metric does not establish uniform superiority.
- **Proved** requires a proof and its assumptions; **discovered** or **first** requires defensible novelty assessment.
- **Led, founded, coauthored, mentored** require the corresponding personal ownership and participation evidence.
- **Certified, approved, authorized** must be restricted to actual parties with authority or formally completed procedures.
- **Ensured, guaranteed, eliminated** require exceptionally complete evidence and should ordinarily be replaced by measured, checked, reduced risk or documented.

## Numeric and scientific guardrails

A scientific number needs the quantity, unit, denominator, time horizon, experimental design, baseline and uncertainty status when materially relevant. Distinguish percentage-point changes from relative percentages. Retain numerical precision consistent with source evidence.

Never conflate:
- physical conservation law with chosen constitutive model;
- theoretical derivation with simulation or experiment;
- model calibration with independent validation;
- solver convergence with accurate physical prediction;
- numerical reproducibility with external field validity;
- correlation with a demonstrated causal mechanism;
- connected electrical systems with verified thermal interdependency;
- parameter uncertainty with complete predictive uncertainty;
- feasibility with proven optimality;
- source-reported result with the user's original result;
- shared project achievement with individual authorship.

**Negative findings matter.** If RMSE improves but MAE worsens, state both. If uncertainty coverage fails, report the observed coverage and what the bands omit. If the equipment connection is unverified, keep the proposed interdependency conditional.

## Genre adaptation

### Thesis, scientific manuscript and research report
Use: research question → physical and mathematical method → evidence and data → result → model limitations → defensible implication. Distinguish original equations from source-derived templates. Cite original source locations whenever available.

### CV and resume
Prefer short accomplishment lines with an accurate leading action verb, specific method, and demonstrable result. Do not claim a completed intervention, publication, impact or award from a proposal or nomination.

### Statement of purpose and scholarship writing
Use the author's natural reasoning: motivation → decision → action → evidence → learning → research question → academic fit. Do not make a personal statement read as a sequence of CV bullets. Use the user's [Authentic Voice skill](../dossiya-authentic-voice/SKILL.md) and [Graduate SOP skill](../graduate-sop/SKILL.md) when applicable.

### Engineering and GitHub
Mark features as IMPLEMENTED, TESTED, PLANNED, BLOCKED or UNKNOWN according to repository code, execution logs and results. Linked source research does not prove the harness implemented a capability. Distinguish synthetic fixtures from real field evidence.

### Teaching, community and leadership
Distinguish volunteered, assisted, coordinated, taught, mentored, founded and led. Include counts, dates and documented effects. Describe team achievements with correct attribution.

### Emails and everyday professional communication
Use natural direct verbs such as confirmed, requested, submitted, attached or clarified. Do not force achievement language into ordinary communication.

## Protected wording and edit-scope rules

- Preserve every passage declared immutable verbatim, including spelling and punctuation. No stylistic or grammatical correction is authorized without express permission.
- Modify only the sentence, paragraph, section or type of change explicitly requested.
- Do not silently change chronology, actors, numerical results, citations or scientific status.
- Avoid em dashes in new prose; protected source passages retain their original punctuation.
- When a change would improve style but exceed the authorized scope, do not make it.
- Distinguish applicant-provided facts from externally verified records and team contributions from sole authorship.

## Examples of properly bounded thesis wording

These are **examples based on previously reported research**, not newly verified experimental results.

**Transformer modeling:** Evaluated a first-order transformer thermal model using 8,592 SINTEF observations, reducing holdout top-oil RMSE from 0.2517 °C for persistence to 0.2165 °C, while documenting worse MAE.

**Thermal memory:** Quantified model-based load-ordering sensitivity across 106 separate 24-hour windows, observing a median absolute final-temperature difference of 0.355 °C under reordered current histories; no EV charging intervention or service improvement was established.

**Uncertainty:** Diagnosed undercoverage of nominal 90% parameter-only prediction bands, which covered approximately 8% of holdout observations in the reported experiment; the bands omitted other predictive uncertainty sources.

**Data qualification:** Audited 16 candidate datasets for physical variables and potential modeling relevance; the available records did not establish synchronized transformer–EVSE thermal causality.

**Research scope:** Formulated candidate directional thermal mechanisms while identifying the equipment-specific observations required for qualification; do not describe proposed coupling as validated.

## Delivery quality gate

- [ ] Wording uses a verb justified by actual evidence and ownership.
- [ ] Each meaningful number retains its correct quantity, unit, denominator, baseline and time range.
- [ ] Mixed or adverse results are visible.
- [ ] A numerical verification is not misrepresented as physical validation.
- [ ] Simulation is not presented as an observed field intervention.
- [ ] Novelty, causal, professional-approval and optimization claims meet their evidence gates.
- [ ] Source provenance and citations are traceable; conflicting evidence is flagged.
- [ ] The output preserves protected text and requested edit boundaries.
- [ ] The writer's authentic voice, chronology and personal reasoning are preserved.
- [ ] The draft is accurate, concise and appropriate to the audience.
