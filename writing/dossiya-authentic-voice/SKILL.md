## The reasoning signature
For social and leadership writing:
`Lived context → Observed need → Personal choice → Action → Lesson → Contribution → Next step`.

For academic and research writing:
`Scientific problem → Personal task → Methods/evidence → What the evidence showed → Limitation or unresolved question → Required mathematical training → Next research direction`.

For a personal or scholarship story:
`Actual circumstances → Decision under constraint → Concrete step → Opportunity → Learning → Responsibility`.

Do not force every step into every paragraph. Use this as a reasoning check, not formulaic writing.

## Stylistic guardrails
1. Preserve simple and concrete first-person English. Correct grammar; do not replace thought with artificial eloquence.
2. Preserve Dossiya's actual chronology. In 2016 he pursued a government scholarship because the available general-secondary path was unaffordable; his interest in engineering developed through technical study, not from an invented childhood scientific obsession.
3. Show why before describing prestige. Avoid lists of scholarships, organizations or programs as a substitute for scientific reasoning.
4. Favor specific action verbs: `applied`, `traveled`, `screened`, `modeled`, `tested`, `analyzed`, `learned`, only when supported.
5. Quantify only verifiable personal achievements. Distinguish selection vs participation, team vs individual effort, planned vs completed activity, output vs measured impact, source claims vs externally checked facts.
6. Reflect on gaps honestly; do not manufacture an epiphany, quote, emotion, mathematical discovery or result.
7. Thank others meaningfully where relevant, but academic statements should not become long acknowledgment lists.
8. Never reuse alarming generalizations about all African youth as objective fact. Frame them as contextual concerns or specific observations.
9. Public-post diction such as `thrilled`, `honored`, `amazing opportunity`, `resounding success`, emojis and hashtags is not the mandatory academic voice. In selective academic writing favor the quieter reflective voice.
10. Preserve scope: the posts reflect public writing in French and English across years; they do not prove all private beliefs or every capability.

## Natural writing, human authorship and anti-robotic revision

**Purpose.** Improve readability while protecting the writer's own ideas and distinctive sentence habits. This is **not** an AI-detector-evasion procedure. Never claim a passage is human-written because it scores well on a detector or uses certain surface features. Apply the receiving institution's current AI-use and authorship policy before offering drafting or rewriting assistance.

### 1. Diagnose before editing

Check a draft against Dossiya's original materials, not against generic "human writing" recipes. Look for these *possible* symptoms, not an automatic word blacklist:
- **Abstract praise instead of evidence:** "transformative journey," "unwavering passion," "prestigious institution," "uniquely positioned" where a precise action or observation should appear.
- **Generic reflection after every event:** a repeated formula of challenge -> breakthrough -> passion -> impact, regardless of what really happened.
- **Invisible actor:** "valuable insights were gained," "research was conducted," where the applicant's own role is established and matters. Passive constructions remain appropriate when the agent is unknown or unimportant.
- **Artificial fluency:** long strings of polished abstractions that are less clear than Dossiya's direct question, action and interpretation.
- **Manufactured rhythm:** identical sentence openings, lengths or transitions; or arbitrary sentence variation introduced only to sound less like AI.
- **Excessive caution or self-promotion:** repeatedly stating a model is unvalidated or repeatedly announcing commitment instead of presenting new evidence.
- **Thesaurus substitutions and decorative technical jargon:** words with little explanatory work, especially where they alter scientific meaning.

A sentence is not weak merely because it is fluent, formal, short, complex, or widely used. Judge its purpose, accuracy and fit with the writer's original reasoning.

### 2. Preserve the writer's thought before fixing the language

1. **Identify the source thought.** Compare to Dossiya's actual first-person writing, research records, or user-supplied draft. Do not manufacture interpretations.
2. **Name the real event or task.** Keep documented details, personal choices, and uncertainty. Avoid rewriting a whole paragraph when one vague sentence can be deleted.
3. **Explain only what the record warrants.** Differentiate observation from interpretation, course exposure from independent work, and unresolved questions from findings.
4. **Make minimal, visible edits.** Repair grammar, sentence connection and redundant words without replacing his vocabulary, meaning or chronology. For important changes show original wording and a proposed *light* edit, or mark what the author needs to rewrite.
5. **Use natural rhythm for meaning.** Mix shorter conclusions with longer technical reasoning when useful. Do not impose sentence-length quotas or make every paragraph end with an inspirational lesson.
6. **Read aloud and review with the author.** Ask: "Would I actually say this?", "Is this my conclusion?", "What evidence supports it?", "Could this sentence describe any applicant?" If yes to the last question, look for concrete original material instead.
7. **Stop when the writing is clear and truthful.** Never intentionally insert grammatical errors, colloquialisms, random synonyms or invented personal anecdotes to make it seem less machine-produced.

**Original-voice anchors:** Dossiya's archived questions on energy and digital work (post 04), his explicit self-assessment and remaining learning needs (posts 02/04), the actual 2019 bus journey to apply for a scholarship (post 09), and his stepwise approach (post 17). His original posts are evidence of his expressions, **not** independent verification of events. Keep correct English; do not imitate mistakes in earlier French or English writing.

### 3. Specialized review procedure

For each paragraph, evaluate separately:
- **Ownership:** Is the action, decision or reflection genuinely Dossiya's?
- **Specificity:** What actual problem, method, observation or choice distinguishes it?
- **Scientific precision:** Are states, limitations, quantities and results represented correctly?
- **Voice fidelity:** Did an editor make him sound unlike his actual words?
- **Necessity:** Does the paragraph advance the argument, or simply restate motivation?
- **Rhythm and clarity:** Is there needless repetition, jargon or a sequence of identical sentence structures?

Provide concise annotations **[KEEP]**, **[CLARIFY]**, **[VERIFY]**, **[CUT]** or **[LIGHT EDIT]**. Recommend the *smallest authorized revision* first. Do not use a numeric "AI-likeness" score, automated style metric, or detector to prove authorship.

## Stanford CME-MS: Evidence-Constrained SOP Optimization

**Scope and precedence.** Apply this Stanford-only method when planning, drafting, reviewing, or revising Dossiya's Stanford CME-MS Statement of Purpose (SOP). It extends the existing authentic-voice and evidence instructions without replacing them. **Factual accuracy, research integrity, and the applicant's own voice always override an editorial score.** Read the actual application prompt for the relevant intake before editing.

### 1. Objective function: editorial heuristic, not admissions scoring

For a candidate statement S, seek a strong evidence-supported version:

```text
S* = argmax J(S)
J(S) = 0.24 E(S) + 0.20 M(S) + 0.18 Q(S)
     + 0.14 F(S) + 0.12 D(S) + 0.07 V(S) + 0.05 C(S)
```

Normalize each dimension to [0, 1]:
- **E (24%):** research evidence and Dossiya's own contribution.
- **M (20%):** demonstrated mathematical readiness, not mere course listings.
- **Q (18%):** precise, coherent scientific and mathematical questions.
- **F (14%):** verified Stanford-specific program and research fit.
- **D (12%):** clear need for mathematical study beyond ASU/WQU graduate work.
- **V (7%):** authentic direct first-person voice and reasoning.
- **C (5%):** organization, clarity, brevity, and non-repetition.

**These are independent editorial weights, not Stanford's rubric, and cannot estimate admission probability.** Apply the hard constraints first.

### 2. Hard constraints, with precedence over J(S)

- **C1:** SOP word count <=1,000 (or stricter actual prompt); target ~950–980 when possible. Report a verified count.
- **C2:** No unsupported factual claims or undocumented generalizations.
- **C3:** No invented numerical results, experimental findings, papers, publications, outcomes, or quantitative impacts.
- **C4:** No false attribution of team or supervisor results to Dossiya.
- **C5:** No unverified Stanford course access, faculty match, research placement, funding, admission or degree-policy statements.
- **C6:** No contradictions with official educational documents, syllabi or primary research records.
- **C7:** Keep **completed**, **ongoing**, and **proposed** research distinct. Do not describe a proposed model as validated.
- **C8:** Remove repetition, generic prestige language, and empty claims of passion.
- **C9:** Preserve the user's exact intended meaning, personal chronology, and straightforward wording.
- **C10:** Avoid em dashes, invented motivations, exaggerated accomplishments, artificial dramatic turning points and borrowed applicant phrasing.
- **C11:** Do not represent numerical convergence as physical validation, assumptions as established facts, or a literature corpus as experimental data.
- **C12:** Never sacrifice accuracy or authentic voice to increase the editorial score.

If a constraint fails, flag and correct it **before** optimizing style.

### 3. Evidence ledger

For every substantive claim, establish:

```text
Claim = {statement, source, personal_contribution,
         evidence_status, research_status, relevance}
evidence_status = VERIFIED | SELF_REPORTED | PROPOSED | UNKNOWN
research_status = COMPLETED | ONGOING | PROPOSED | NOT_ESTABLISHED
```

- **VERIFIED:** a transcript, official record, syllabus, research artifact, original assignment, dataset or other reliable source supports the claim **within its actual scope**.
- **SELF_REPORTED:** supported by Dossiya's personal account or public writing, not independently audited.
- **PROPOSED:** future study, mathematical question, research activity, graduation, or career intention.
- **UNKNOWN:** missing, ambiguous, inconsistent or unverified; omit, qualify or seek records.
- Record individual action versus team contribution. Distinguish what was screened, read, extracted, measured, published, and implemented.
- A course title on a transcript does **not** prove mastery of every syllabus topic. An in-progress assessment does not establish a final grade.
- Preserve differing dates/degree terminology across official records until reconciled; do not silently harmonize.

### 4. Scientific reasoning algorithm for each research experience

Extract **P-A-H-E-L-N**:
1. **P**: precise scientific problem.
2. **A**: Dossiya's personal task, choice, and action.
3. **H**: method, hypothesis, modeling assumption, or evidence standard.
4. **E**: actual evidence, output or supported result.
5. **L**: limitation, uncertainty, or unresolved problem.
6. **N**: the mathematical training needed for the next stage.

Use the logic **P -> A -> H -> E -> L -> N** without forcing all six into every paragraph. A research paragraph fails if it cannot distinguish personal contribution from literature context or if it does not explain what remains unknown. Prefer one rigorous example to a list of unrelated accomplishments.

### 5. Central mathematical research direction

Primary focus: **mathematical and numerical study of coupled power–electrified-transport systems under extreme environmental conditions.**

Candidate quantities include transformer thermal states, time-varying charging load, ambient temperature, operational limits, unknown parameters, and charging-service consequences. A **proposed abstract formulation**, not an established result, is:

```text
dx/dt = f(x, u, T_a; theta)
```

Questions to justify with science and evidence:
- Which physical states are required, and how are the interfaces coupled?
- What assumptions, boundary conditions, constraints and parameters are defensible?
- What parameters are observable or identifiable?
- When are solutions or operating regimes stable?
- How sensitive are predictions to uncertainty and parameter choice?
- How do numerical approximation, time-stepping and convergence affect results?
- What synchronized measurements would validate the physical model?

**Methodological distinctions:** ODE vs PDE models; physical mechanisms vs correlation; mathematical stability vs physical validation; numerical convergence vs predictive accuracy; future hypotheses vs completed proofs. Do not impose a PDE model on a lumped transformer problem unless spatial physics warrants it. Do not add inverse problems, physics-informed ML, optimization, or SDEs merely because they appear in planned courses. In the SOP, favor accessible scientific description over automatic equation insertion.

3. Locate contradictions, unsupported assertions, research-method gaps and hidden causal leaps.
