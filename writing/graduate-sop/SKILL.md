# Evidence-Led Graduate SOP Skill

## When to use
Use for requests to **plan**, **draft**, **review**, **grade**, or **revise** a graduate Statement of Purpose (SOP). Works for master's and doctoral applications. Read `references/STANFORD_CME_MS.md` for Dossiya's Stanford Computational and Mathematical Engineering (CME) MS application. For writing in Dossiya's first-person voice, **first read** `../dossiya-authentic-voice/SKILL.md` and relevant original writing samples.

**This skill is a drafting and editorial aid, not an admission predictor or an official Stanford rubric.**

## Priority of instructions
1. Exact current prompt, word/character limit, and authorship policy from the user's actual application.
2. Current program website and institutional admissions rules.
3. Original records: transcripts, coursework, research reports, code, supervisor feedback, and applicant's own text.
4. User-supplied institutional writing guidance, including Stanford's advice.
5. This reusable skill and its program adapter.

For **Knight-Hennessy Scholars** applications, follow the separate, more restrictive AI-use policy already recorded in the authentic-voice skill. Do not carry this skill's permission to generate an ordinary SOP into an application where that is not allowed.

## Step 1: Parse the application prompt
Create a requirement map before writing. For Stanford's general graduate SOP, address:
- **Why apply:** concrete intellectual motivation and why graduate study is needed.
- **Preparation:** relevant coursework, methods, independent work, and research skills.
- **Research interests:** specific questions, approaches, and open uncertainties.
- **Future plans:** credible next academic or professional steps.
- **Background:** only experiences that help explain preparation, perspective, or motivation.

Check the *actual* program prompt because it may differ. Stanford's general graduate guidance recommends a **maximum of 1,000 words**; follow any stricter program/portal limit. A graduate certificate is a different application product: the source guidance describes a 4,000-character statement, but verify its current portal instructions before applying that limit. Never import the master's SOP format into a certificate application.

## Step 2: Define one central argument
Write a one-sentence thesis:
`Because I encountered [specific research/technical problem], used [documented methods], and found [specific unresolved issue], I need [program capabilities] to pursue [future research or career goal].`

This is a **planning test**, not text to paste formulaically. Every substantial paragraph must either demonstrate preparation, deepen the research question, justify the program, or explain direction. Remove paragraph-length résumé repetition.

## Step 3: Select and audit evidence
Before drafting, maintain a claim ledger:

```text
claim | original source | my action | team/supervisor action |
evidence class | research stage | relevance to program | wording permitted
```

Evidence class: `DOCUMENTED`, `SELF-REPORTED`, `PROPOSED`, or `UNVERIFIED`.
Research stage: `COMPLETED`, `ONGOING`, `PROPOSED`, or `NOT ESTABLISHED`.

Rules:
- Do not invent results, publications, mentor statements, metrics, or personal emotions.
- Distinguish a **group** contribution from individual implementation or analysis.
- Distinguish numerical convergence from physical validation; statistical association from causation; literature screening from experimentation.
- Do not represent an in-progress grade as final, or a proposed thesis result as completed.
- Course titles alone do not prove mastery; show the mathematical task or problem solved.
- For any externally relevant statistic, use a verifiable source and date.

Prioritize **two or three revealing accomplishments**, not the largest possible list of awards. Prefer a task, choice, method, result, and limitation over generic claims of passion.

## Step 4: Build the research story
For a scientific experience, extract these facts without forcing a six-sentence template:
1. **Problem:** What precise question or limitation motivated the work?
2. **Action:** What did the applicant personally do or decide?
3. **Method:** Which method, assumption, data, equation, or experiment was involved?
4. **Evidence:** What can the records actually establish?
5. **Limitation:** What was not established, or what new question remained?
6. **Next training:** What conceptual or methodological preparation is needed?

In a research SOP, link methods to meaning. A number alone is not a contribution. Explain why it matters scientifically and what it does *not* show.

## Step 5: Structure for meaning, not a rigid chronology
Suggested progression, adjustable to the actual prompt:
1. **Opening:** an authentic scientific question, observation, or experience; state the intended field and purpose early.
2. **Foundation:** selective education and preparation that explain the skills already acquired.
3. **Evidence:** research and technical experiences organized around the central question, each with applicant-owned action.
4. **Gap:** one clearly expressed limitation in current preparation, knowledge, or methods.
5. **Fit:** verified courses, research directions, faculty work, facilities, or program structure that specifically address that gap.
6. **Future:** feasible near-term research aims and longer-term intellectual or professional contribution.

An anecdote, quote, or dramatic hook is **optional**, never required. Use one only if authentic and relevant. Personal circumstances may explain an educational decision, but must not eclipse the program's academic criteria. Where a separate community statement exists, do not duplicate its whole narrative in the SOP.

## Step 6: Establish program-specific fit
Use `research need -> specific program opportunity -> how it advances my work`.

- Verify faculty current appointments and research, course descriptions, prerequisites, program track, research arrangements, and deadlines using current official sources.
- Show why **this program** rather than just its prestige.
- Do not promise access to a professor, lab, research project, restricted class, funding, or a course that may not be offered.
- If pursuing an additional master's, directly distinguish existing degrees and skills from the specific training sought. A second degree must add intellectual purpose, not merely another credential.
- Customize each statement; retain accurate personal evidence but replace the program-fit logic.

## Step 7: Write concisely in the applicant's voice
Adopt the applicant's actual thinking and sentence habits. Use clear first-person verbs and careful claims. Prefer:
- `I screened [number] papers and compared [method]. This showed [supported finding].`
over
- `My transformative journey ignited an unwavering passion for groundbreaking research.`

Avoid: prestige flattery, invented epiphanies, stock quotations, decorative equations, unsupported superlatives, empty transitions, the em dash character, and paragraphs that end with the same generic lesson. Do not deliberately add mistakes or imitate a writing detector. Correct grammar while preserving meaning.

## Step 8: Edit in layers
1. **Truth:** confirm every substantive claim and its status.
2. **Ownership:** clarify I/we, original work versus cited or supervised work.
3. **Prompt:** identify where each required element is addressed.
4. **Scientific logic:** explain why each experience leads to the next intellectual question.
5. **Program fit:** verify what is available, eligible, and relevant.
6. **Voice:** retain the author's genuine decisions and reflections.
7. **Concision:** cut repetition, duplicate awards, and unexplained technical detail.
8. **Proofreading:** check terminology, degree titles, dates, syntax, and exact word/character count.

Have a trusted mentor or faculty reader assess scientific accuracy and readability where feasible. Stop editing when each paragraph has a purpose and further polish would weaken voice or accuracy.

## Review protocol
Mark each weakness as `[KEEP]`, `[VERIFY]`, `[CLARIFY]`, `[CUT]`, or `[LIGHT EDIT]`.
Audit these seven dimensions:
- Compliance with prompt and length
- Academic and technical preparation
- Evidence and applicant ownership
- Coherent research direction and limits
- Specific program fit
- Plausible future plans
- Authentic, concise prose

Do not assign admission probabilities or present editorial scores as admissions committee scores. Critical failures (fabricated results, false attribution, wrong program, unsupported research claims, or limit violations) must be resolved before style optimization.

## Response modes
- **PLAN:** prompt requirement map, one-sentence argument, evidence priorities, paragraph plan, evidence gaps.
- **DRAFT:** a complete statement within the applicable limit, with real facts only and a reported word count.
- **REVIEW:** criterion-by-criterion audit, the three to five highest-impact issues, textual evidence, and a revision plan.
- **REVISE:** complete revised statement plus concise explanation of changes, exact word count, and unresolved factual checks.

If there is no supplied draft and the user's application prohibits substantive AI writing, **do not** use DRAFT or REVISE; restrict assistance to what that institution allows.
