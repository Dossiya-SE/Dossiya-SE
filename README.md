## Professional profile

My research direction is **mathematics-first**. I am interested in how ideas from differential geometry, graph theory, topology, analysis, dynamical systems, probability and optimization can be used to formulate difficult engineering questions involving interdependence, disturbance propagation, recovery, viability, resilience and sustainability.

The translation from mathematics to engineering is treated explicitly:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Abstract mathematical structure}
\xrightarrow{\text{interpretation}}
\text{engineering state space}
\xrightarrow{\text{governing model}}
\text{dynamics + constraints}
\xrightarrow{\text{computation}}
\text{verification + validation}
\xrightarrow{}
\text{engineering decision}
}
```

A mathematical analogy is **not** automatically a physical model. An engineering interpretation requires defined state variables, units, assumptions, coupling laws, admissible controls, boundary/initial conditions, uncertainty, evidence and a stated validity domain.

## Mathematical foundations and methods

| Mathematical area | Objects / questions | Engineering translation under study |
|---|---|---|
| **Differential geometry** | manifolds, tangent structure, metrics, geodesics, curvature | geometry of constrained system states and resilience margins |
| **Graph theory & topology** | multilayer graphs, connectivity, interfaces, structural relations | interdependent infrastructure architecture and propagation pathways |
| **Analysis & differential equations** | state evolution, stability, trajectories, continuous dynamics | disturbance propagation, recovery and coupled system evolution |
| **Dynamical systems** | equilibria, stability, bifurcation, multiscale behavior | transitions between operational, degraded and recovery regimes |
| **Probability & uncertainty** | random variables, stochastic processes, posterior uncertainty | hazards, uncertain parameters, reliability and model risk |
| **Optimization & control** | feasible sets, objectives, constraints, admissible controls | intervention, restoration and resource-allocation decisions |

The purpose is not to decorate engineering problems with advanced mathematics. The purpose is to determine **which mathematical structures are valid, useful and testable for a given engineering system**.

## Research standard

I distinguish between five levels that are often incorrectly collapsed:

```text
mathematical definition
        ≠
mathematical model
        ≠
numerical result
        ≠
software verification
        ≠
empirical validation
```

Accordingly:

- a mathematically elegant construction is not automatically a physically meaningful mechanism;
- passing computational tests establishes implementation properties, not empirical truth;
- engineering conclusions should state assumptions, uncertainty, evidence and validity limits;
- stronger mathematical language requires stronger definitions, not stronger rhetoric.
