The profile should present a cumulative engineering-to-mathematics trajectory rather than a disconnected list of diplomas and projects.


## 3. Long-term research proposition

The profile should communicate the following bounded ambition:

> **Investigate whether rigorous mathematical structures can become reusable tools for describing, analyzing, visualizing and improving sustainable resilience across sectors—energy, infrastructure, finance, climate, resource systems, mobility, digital systems and other complex socio-technical domains.**

The ambition is intentionally broad, but the scientific claim is narrow:

```text
transferable mathematical framework = research question
not established universal theory
```

The analogy with AI is about **cross-sector reusability**:

- AI provides reusable computational capabilities across many application domains;
- this research programme asks whether mathematical representations of state, geometry, coupling, constraints, uncertainty, viability, recovery and decision can likewise form reusable cross-sector analytical infrastructure.

The profile must never imply that differential geometry alone can solve every sustainability problem.

## 6. Public narrative boundaries

### Strong claims that are appropriate

- multi-stage progression from electrical engineering to energy, sustainability, quantitative finance and mathematical research;
- interdisciplinary training across physical, engineered and stochastic systems;
- active research interest in mathematical physics, geometry, resilience and scientific visualization;
- ambition to build reusable mathematical frameworks for sustainable/resilient systems;
- evidence-first distinction between models, simulations, prototypes and validated real-world claims.

### Claims to avoid without stronger evidence

- “expert in every field listed”;
- “differential geometry will solve sustainability”;
- “universal resilience theory”;
- “AI-equivalent technology”;
- “validated across every sector”;
- completion of ongoing master's degrees;
- official credential names that have not been checked against the diploma text.

## 7. Profile hierarchy

The recommended public hierarchy is:

1. **Who I am now** — engineer / quantitative researcher with a systems-and-mathematics trajectory.
2. **Where I came from** — practical electrical engineering and energy systems.
3. **What I am studying** — sustainable engineering and financial engineering.
4. **What I research** — coupled systems, resilience, uncertainty, mathematical structure and computation.
5. **Where I am going** — deeper discrete/differential geometry and mathematical art for cross-sector sustainable resilience.
6. **What evidence exists** — repositories, models, code, reports, validation gates and publications when available.
