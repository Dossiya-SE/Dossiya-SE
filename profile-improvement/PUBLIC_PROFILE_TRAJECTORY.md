## Why the two graduate programmes belong in one profile

Sustainable engineering contributes questions of:

- coupled infrastructure and physical systems;
- sustainability constraints;
- resilience and recovery;
- life-cycle/system boundaries;
- feasibility, equity and implementation.

Financial engineering contributes methods for:

- probability and stochastic processes;
- econometrics and statistical inference;
- simulation;
- optimization;
- risk and uncertainty;
- model validation;
- sequential/causal decision analysis.

The profile therefore treats financial engineering as additional quantitative training for complex uncertain systems, not as an unrelated career detour.

## Mathematics research direction

The forward mathematical programme is deliberately plural rather than based on one technique:

```text
geometry
+ dynamical systems
+ network science
+ topology/state-space structure
+ inverse problems
+ uncertainty quantification
+ optimization/control
+ scientific computing
+ mathematical visualization
```

Differential geometry is a major research direction, but it is not presented as a universal solution. Discrete differential geometry is particularly relevant where the objects are graphs, meshes, networks or discretized physical structures.

## Cross-sector ambition

The bounded research question is:

> **Which mathematical structures are genuinely transferable across different complex systems, and under what domain-specific assumptions can they improve representation, analysis, visualization, resilience assessment or decision-making?**

Potential application domains include energy, infrastructure, finance, climate adaptation, resource systems, mobility, digital systems and other coupled socio-technical systems.

The comparison with AI is limited to one property: **cross-domain reusability**. It does not imply that a mathematical framework has AI-like generality, autonomy or universal applicability.
